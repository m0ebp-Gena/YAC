Nexus engine for the YAC Windows audio-channel experiment

Use Jimmy Next v2.0.88 (c38093aa928cba96c5f8ecc77481c6fd6b8da2b3).
https://github.com/Jimr9/JimmyNext/tree/v2.0.88

Prepare the Nexus checkout with Jimmy Next's scripts/prepare-nexus.ps1.
Its pin is Nexus v1.21.0 (a5777aef0797c78decb8d4cdbc1dfefb34bcd44f).
https://github.com/kd9taw/Nexus/tree/a5777aef0797c78decb8d4cdbc1dfefb34bcd44f

Apply jimmy-input-channel.patch at the Jimmy Next root. Apply
nexus-input-channel.patch at the prepared EngineHost/.nexus-src root.
Build EngineHost with cargo build --release --locked, using the GNU target
and MSYS2 UCRT64 packages listed in Jimmy Next's CI workflow. Stage the
engine and matching runtime DLLs as shown in that workflow.

An unmodified engine can still use a mono device. For a multichannel input,
YAC checks the patched host's capability and passes the saved one-based
channel in YAC_NEXUS_INPUT_CHANNEL to that child process. The patched
capture callback uses that channel before resampling. With the variable
absent the upstream engine retains its usual mono downmix.
