FT8 and FT4 reception on Windows

The experimental decoder uses the bundled Jimmy Next EngineHost and Nexus.
It reads the radio audio and collects received messages. The YAC interface
supports reception only. FT8 and FT4 decoding is available on Windows.

At first run, enter your amateur radio callsign at Callsign and confirm with y.
If you want distance estimates, answer y to the Maidenhead locator question.
Enter a four- or six-character locator and press Enter. Press y to save it or n
to revise the entry. If you already use YAC, set ft_grid in your yac.ini.

At Top Level, press d then a. Select the radio audio input with full stop or
comma, choose the channel with c and press Enter to save. In yac.ini, set
ft_engine=_internal/nexus/jimmy-engine-host.exe and set ft_audio_output to the
exact name of a separate non-radio playback device, such as your PC speakers.
From the YAC folder, the following PowerShell command lists the playback names
accepted by the engine.

.\_internal\nexus\jimmy-engine-host.exe --list-output-devices

For the first decoder test, tune your radio to the desired FT8 or FT4 frequency
and set the appropriate data mode. At Top Level, press d then f. Press m to
switch between FT8 and FT4, then s to start decoding. Press v to read the latest
result. Use comma or full stop to review earlier or later results. Press c to
clear the results, or h for help.

Press f inside the decoder to open the filter selector. Full stop and comma
choose All Messages, Calling CQ or Distance. Press e, then k for kilometres or
m for miles, to edit the distance cutoff. Enter saves and selects Distance.
Blank Enter aborts. Press q to return to the results. Distance is estimated
from the grids in the message and your own locator.

Mode announcements

Mode selection and radio status now say names such as Data USB, Data LSB and
FM Narrow. C4FM, FT8 and FT4 retain their usual spellings.

Verification

Live Windows FT8 reception and distance review were exercised on an FTX-1.
The packaged Windows startup and locator confirmation were checked with a
simulated radio. The mode announcement checks used simulated CAT and CI-V
responses. The Mac/Linux package contains the shared interface changes.
